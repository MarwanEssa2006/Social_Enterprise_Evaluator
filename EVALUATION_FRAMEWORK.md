## 1. Introduction and Purpose

This document defines **how the platform interprets and assesses** a social enterprise against the eight approved criteria of Alan's List of Success, using the approved question bank.

It defines, for each criterion independently:
- what the criterion measures (interpretation);
- what each approved answer indicates (answer interpretation);
- how answers combine into a qualitative assessment (assessment logic);
- how evidence is judged and how confidence is formed (evidence evaluation);
- how unknown, missing, and conflicting information is handled;
- the qualitative outcomes available;
- how improvement recommendations are generated.

It does **not** define numerical scoring. Phase 3 will design the scoring methodology on top of the qualitative readings defined here.

**The enterprise model being evaluated [APPROVED]:** a social enterprise must achieve both (1) financial profitability, sustainable revenue, a viable business model, and the ability to cover costs and generate profit; and (2) meaningful, measurable social impact for a clearly defined target population with a real social need, with sustainable benefits. Neither objective replaces the other. Grants and donations may supplement revenue but are never, alone, proof of profitability or sustainability.

## 4. Shared Evaluation Structure

Every criterion is evaluated through the same seven-part structure, with criterion-specific content in each part.

```
 Approved answers (Q1–Q4 [+ LM evidence follow-up]) + supporting evidence
          │
          ▼
 (1) Answer interpretation ─ each answer classified (4.1)
          │
          ▼
 (2) Dimension readings ─ Commercial reading (Q1+Q2), Social reading (Q3+Q4) (4.2)
          │                 criterion-specific rules
          ▼
 (3) Evidence confidence ─ per dimension, separate from readings (4.3)
          │
          ▼
 (4) Information flags ─ Unknown / Not yet determined / Unanswered / Conflicting (4.4)
          │
          ▼
 (5) Criterion outcome ─ qualitative label (4.5); both dimension readings stay visible
          │
          ▼
 (6) Recommendations ─ performance-improvement and evidence-strengthening, per dimension (4.6)
```

### 4.0 Project Context — Basic Project Information [APPROVED]

The following items are **basic project information**. They are **not evaluation questions**, are not scored themselves, and do not change the wording, order, or options of the approved question bank. They provide the context needed to interpret the answers.

#### 4.0.1 Target Geography [APPROVED]

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Target country | The country in which the enterprise operates and serves its customers and beneficiaries. |
| Target geographic area | The area within that country to which the estimates refer (for example a governorate, city, district, or region). Exact field format is a Phase 5 design detail (FDD-04). |

Use in this framework:
- **Large Market (C1):** Q2 ("النطاق الجغرافي المستهدف") and Q4 ("المنطقة المستهدفة") are read as referring to the recorded target area. Size bands B1–B5 are interpreted relative to that area (Phase 3 calibration). Evidence is checked for relevance to that country and area (e.g., national figures used for a local area are flagged).
- If the target country or area is missing, Large Market keeps the existing rule: the readings are provisional, confidence cannot exceed Low, and the flag "Insufficient information — geography" is raised. Missing geography is never treated as a small market or small need.

#### 4.0.2 Beneficiary Model [APPROVED decision; application rules PROPOSED]

Each project records how its beneficiaries take part in the enterprise:

| Model | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Workers / producers / earners** | Beneficiaries take part in production, work, or service delivery and earn income from the enterprise. |
| **Service users** | Beneficiaries receive the product or service (as recipients or users) and are not expected to work in or earn from the enterprise. |
| **Both** | Some beneficiaries work/earn and some are service users, or the same beneficiaries do both. |
| **Not yet determined** | The founder has not defined the model yet. |

All four model options are approved (D-18, D-21).

The model is a founder statement with a short description of how beneficiaries benefit. The Evidence Evaluator assesses it like any other claim.

**Applicability of social dimensions.** Some approved social questions presuppose that beneficiaries work, produce, or earn. Under the **Service users** model those social dimensions are genuinely inapplicable and are marked **Not Applicable**. The mapping is fixed:

| Criterion | Social dimension (as worded in the approved bank) | Workers / producers / earners | Service users | Both | Not yet determined |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 Large Market | Beneficiary population size (social need) | Applicable | **Applicable** | Applicable | Applicable |
| C2 High Gross Margins | Beneficiary income vs time and effort | Applicable | Not Applicable | Applicable | Applicable |
| C3 Recurring Revenue | Continuity of beneficiary employment and income | Applicable | Not Applicable | Applicable | Applicable |
| C4 Leverage Our Capabilities | Beneficiary skills to take part in execution | Applicable | Not Applicable | Applicable | Applicable |
| C5 Supply Chain Capabilities | Beneficiary access to resources to take part | Applicable | Not Applicable | Applicable | Applicable |
| C6 Direct Customer Access | Beneficiary participation in value creation and market | Applicable | Not Applicable | Applicable | Applicable |
| C7 PR & Communications | Beneficiary voice and demonstrable social impact | Applicable | **Applicable** | Applicable | Applicable |
| C8 Exit Strategy | Beneficiary independence: managing, continuing work and income without founders | Applicable | Not Applicable | Applicable | Applicable |

**Rules for Not Applicable (N/A):**
1. **N/A is not weak performance, not zero, not Unknown, and not positive.** It means the approved questions of that dimension do not describe this enterprise's model. Confidence for an N/A dimension is "Not applicable".
2. **N/A is set only by the mapping above.** Neither the founder nor the AI Analyst can mark an individual question or dimension N/A. N/A applies to a whole social dimension (both its questions), never to one question.
3. **The commercial dimension is always applicable.** The model never affects commercial readings.
4. **C1 and C7 social dimensions are always applicable.** Every social enterprise must show a real social need and demonstrable impact, whatever the model.
5. **All 32 questions are always presented**, including those on N/A dimensions; the beneficiary model never hides a question. Every question must be answered before submission, including those on N/A dimensions; "لسه مش عارفين" is an acceptable answer (D-24). Answers given on N/A dimensions are stored and used for the model conflict check only; "لسه مش عارفين" or a stated absence on an N/A dimension does not trigger that check.

**Safeguards so that N/A does not hide missing or negative social impact:**
- **Model conflict check.** Under the Service users model, the expected answers on N/A dimensions are "stated absence" (X) or Unknown (U). If an answer on an N/A dimension indicates that beneficiaries do work, earn, produce, or participate (codes S+, S, L, or W), the flag **Model conflict** is raised and that dimension is treated as **Applicable** and read normally until the model is clarified. A negative answer (e.g., "دخل بسيط مقابل مجهود كبير") therefore remains visible as a negative reading.
- **Not yet determined → all applicable.** An undefined model never produces N/A.
- **Social coverage is reported.** For every project the platform reports how many social dimensions were assessed (e.g., "social dimensions assessed: 2 of 8") and flags **Limited social coverage** when the Service users model applies.
- **Social impact gate.** Under the Service users model the social case rests on C1 social and C7 social. If either is Weak, Absent, Undetermined, or Conflicting, the project-level flag **Social impact not demonstrated** is raised and shown prominently in results and reports.
- **Uncovered aspects are named.** The approved questions do not measure the benefit actually delivered to service users beyond C1 Q3–Q4 and C7 Q3–Q4 (for example continuity or quality of the service to beneficiaries). This gap is shown as a visible note, not filled with assumptions (ISSUE-16).
- **Outcome labels show N/A explicitly** (see §4.5). A criterion with an N/A social dimension is never labelled as if both dimensions were assessed.

### 4.1 Answer Classification Codes [PROPOSED]

Every approved answer option is given one interpretation code. Codes are **qualitative**; they are not scores and imply no numeric distance between levels.

| Code | Meaning | Treated as |
|---|---|---|
| **S+** | Strong positive indication for this question's aspect. | Performance signal |
| **S** | Positive indication. | Performance signal |
| **L** | Limited indication — present but constrained. | Performance signal |
| **W** | Weak or adverse indication. | Performance signal |
| **X** | **Stated absence** — the founder states that a mechanism, plan, channel, capability, or arrangement does **not** currently exist. This is a known current gap, not unknown information. | Performance signal (current state) |
| **ND** | **Not yet determined** — the founder states the matter has not been calculated, tested, or identified yet (e.g., "لم نحسب ذلك بعد", "لم نختبر"). | Information gap + planning-maturity signal; **not** equivalent to W |
| **U** | **Unknown** — "لسه مش عارفين." Information unavailable; requires verification. | Information gap only; **never** a negative performance signal |
| **C** | **Categorical / descriptive** — option describes a type (customer group, channel, pathway) with no inherent better/worse ranking. | Context for interpretation, consistency checks, recommendations |
| **B1–B5** | **Size band** (Large Market Q2 and Q4 only) — ordered magnitude ranges as written in the options. Whether a band counts as "large" depends on geography and context and is a Phase 3 calibration. | Ordered magnitude, not a performance level by itself |

Distinguishing **X**, **ND**, and **U** is essential:
- **U** says nothing about performance.
- **ND** says the work has not been done yet; the performance is unknown but the founder knows it is unknown, which is a planning-maturity observation.
- **X** says the thing does not exist today. This is a current weakness, but it is still a statement of the present state, not proof that the idea cannot work.

### 4.2 Dimension Readings [PROPOSED]

Each dimension of each criterion receives one reading:

| Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Strong** | Both questions in the dimension point clearly to strong performance on this criterion's aspect. |
| **Adequate** | Positive overall, with stated limitations. |
| **Limited** | Present but materially constrained. |
| **Weak** | Adverse indications on the primary aspect. |
| **Absent** | The founder states the essential mechanism does not exist yet (X on the anchor question). |
| **Not yet determined** | The anchor question is ND, or the dimension cannot be read because work has not been done. |
| **Undetermined** | The anchor question is U or unanswered, and the other question cannot substitute for it. |
| **Not Applicable** | Social dimensions only: the dimension does not apply to the recorded beneficiary model (§4.0.2). Not a performance level. |

Each criterion section defines (a) which question is the **anchor** of each dimension, (b) how the second question **qualifies** the anchor, and (c) which combinations are **contradictions**. Answers are never summed or averaged.

### 4.3 Evidence Confidence [PROPOSED]

Evidence confidence is a **separate output** from the dimension reading. It describes how well the reading is supported, not how good the performance is.

#### 4.3.1 Evidence Classes

| Class | Definition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **EV-1 Documented operational evidence** | Records produced by actual activity, traceable to a source. | Invoices, sales records, signed contracts, purchase orders, payment records to beneficiaries, supplier invoices, delivery records. |
| **EV-2 Documented third-party evidence** | Information from an identified external source with date and origin. | Published statistics, research reports, official data, letters of intent from named organisations, supplier quotations. |
| **EV-3 Primary research** | Data collected by the project from the target group with a stated method. | Interviews, surveys, pilot results, focus groups (with sample size, date, method). |
| **EV-4 Estimate** | A figure produced by a stated calculation or reasoning from stated inputs. | Cost sheet estimate, market-size calculation with stated assumptions. |
| **EV-5 Observation** | The founder's personal observation or experience, not systematically collected. | "We saw in our village that…", personal work history. |
| **EV-6 Assumption / expectation** | A claim with no stated basis. | Answer selections without supporting material; "we expect". |
| **Missing** | No supporting evidence for a claim that needs it. | — |
| **Conflicting** | Evidence items, or evidence and answers, that cannot both be true. | — |

Rules:
1. **An answer selection is a founder statement (EV-6 by default)**, not evidence. Many approved questions explicitly ask for expectations ("المتوقع", "تقديركم"). Answers are labelled as expectations or estimates unless supported.
2. **AI extraction does not create evidence.** Statements extracted by the AI Analyst from free text keep their original class and remain linked to the source input.
3. **Documented ≠ independently verified.** The platform can assess whether evidence is documented, relevant, specific, and consistent. It cannot, by itself, confirm that a document is authentic. Labels must say "documented" or "supported by evidence provided", not "verified", unless an independent verification step exists (ISSUE-15).
4. **Relevance is criterion-specific.** Evidence strong for one aspect may be irrelevant for another (e.g., sales records prove paying customers exist, but do not measure total market size).
5. **Grant or donation documents are not evidence of commercial revenue, margin, or recurrence.** They may be recorded as supplementary funding.

#### 4.3.2 Confidence Levels

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **High** | The anchor claims of the dimension are supported by relevant EV-1/EV-2 evidence, or substantial EV-3 evidence, with no unresolved conflict. |
| **Moderate** | Supported by EV-3 or credible EV-4 evidence, or EV-1/EV-2 evidence that covers only part of the claim. |
| **Low** | Supported only by EV-5 observations or estimates without a stated method. |
| **Statement only** | No supporting evidence; the reading rests on answer selections (EV-6). |
| **Not assessable** | The reading is Undetermined or Not yet determined, so there is nothing to have confidence in. |

What makes evidence credible and useful (all criteria): it is **relevant** to the specific claim; **specific** (numbers, names, dates, places); **traceable** (source identified); **recent** enough to describe the current situation; **connected to the target geography and population**; **methodologically explained** (for surveys and estimates: sample, method, assumptions); and **consistent** with other answers and evidence.

Unresolved conflicting evidence on an anchor claim prevents High confidence for that dimension and must be flagged.

Dimension-level confidence defined here is the input to the **criterion-level** and **overall** Evidence Confidence required by D-26. Confidence is displayed separately and never raises or lowers a performance score. How criterion-level and overall confidence are derived is OPEN-34.

### 4.4 Information States and Flags [PROPOSED]

| State | Trigger | Can conclude | Cannot conclude | Required / flagged |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Unanswered** | Question left blank (possible in a saved draft only). | Nothing about that aspect. | That the aspect is weak or strong. | Submission is not accepted until every question is answered; "لسه مش عارفين" is a valid answer (D-24). |
| **Unknown (U)** | "لسه مش عارفين." | The founder lacks this information today. | Any performance level; never zero, failure, or negative. | Flag "requires verification"; recommendation to obtain information. |
| **Not yet determined (ND)** | "Not calculated / not tested / not identified yet." | The analysis has not been done. | The performance level. | Flag "analysis needed"; recommendation to do the analysis. |
| **Stated absence (X)** | "No mechanism / plan / channel exists." | A current gap exists in that mechanism. | That the gap cannot be closed or that the idea fails. | Recommendation to build the mechanism. |
| **Insufficient information** | Answers given but the evidence or detail needed to interpret them (e.g., target geography) is missing. | Provisional reading. | A reliable reading. | Flag specific missing detail. |
| **Missing evidence** | Positive claim without supporting evidence. | The claim stands as a founder statement. | That the claim is supported. | Confidence = Statement only; evidence recommendation. |
| **Conflicting answers** | Answers that cannot both be true (defined per criterion). | That at least one answer is inaccurate. | Which answer is correct. | Flag both answers; no reading is based on the more favourable one by default; request clarification. |
| **Contradictory evidence** | Evidence contradicts an answer or other evidence. | That the claim is contested. | Which version is correct, unless the contradicting evidence is clearly stronger (EV-1/EV-2 over EV-6). | Flag; show both; confidence cannot be High. |
| **Not Applicable** | Social dimension inapplicable under the recorded beneficiary model (§4.0.2). | The dimension's questions do not describe this model. | Any performance level, positive or negative. | Show "Not Applicable" with the reason; apply the safeguards in §4.0.2. |
| **Model conflict** | An answer on an N/A dimension indicates beneficiaries work, earn, produce, or participate. | The recorded model may be wrong for this dimension. | Which is correct. | Treat the dimension as Applicable; ask the founder to confirm the model. |

When answers conflict, the reading must **not** silently adopt the more favourable answer, and must **not** silently adopt the less favourable one. The dimension is marked **Conflicting**, both answers are shown, and the outcome follows the "Significant uncertainty" rule unless the conflict is resolved by evidence.

### 4.5 Criterion Outcomes [PROPOSED]

Each criterion receives one outcome label **plus** its two dimension readings, its evidence confidence, and its flags. The label never hides a dimension reading.

| Outcome | When it is used (general definition; criterion sections add specific triggers) |
|---|---|
| **Strong performance** | Both dimensions Strong or Adequate, at least one Strong; no unresolved conflict; confidence at least Moderate on the anchor claims of both dimensions. |
| **Positive, partially validated** | Both dimensions Strong or Adequate; some documented support exists, but confidence is Low in at least one dimension, or evidence covers only part of the claims. |
| **Potential requiring validation** | Both dimensions read positively (or one positively and one Not yet determined) but the readings rest on statements only or assumptions; **or** the criterion-specific benefit is expected but not yet realised (defined per criterion). |
| **Imbalanced: commercial–social gap** | One dimension Strong/Adequate, the other Limited/Weak/Absent. The label names the weaker dimension. **The stronger dimension does not compensate for the weaker one.** |
| **Weak performance** | Both dimensions Limited, Weak, or Absent, or one Weak/Absent and the other Limited. |
| **Significant uncertainty** | Unresolved conflicting answers or contradictory evidence on the anchor of at least one dimension. |
| **Insufficient information for a reliable conclusion** | At least one dimension Undetermined (and not resolvable from evidence). The readable dimension's reading is still reported and still generates recommendations. |

**Proposed precedence** when several apply: Insufficient information → Significant uncertainty → Imbalanced → Weak → Potential requiring validation → Positive, partially validated → Strong. The precedence is a presentation rule; Phase 3 decides how each output affects scores.

**Confidence is reported next to every outcome**, including Weak outcomes (e.g., "Weak performance — statement only": the founder's own answers indicate weakness, but it is not evidenced).

**Criteria with a Not Applicable social dimension.** The outcome is determined from the commercial dimension alone, using the same labels applied to that one dimension, and always carries the suffix **"— commercial dimension only; social: Not Applicable (service-user model)"**. "Imbalanced" cannot apply. The suffix is shown wherever the outcome appears, including reports. In scoring, the N/A dimension is excluded from the relevant calculation and shown with its reason, and Social Coverage is displayed beside the Social Score (D-25; coverage definition: OPEN-35).

### 4.6 Recommendation Structure [APPROVED content; generation rules PROPOSED]

Every recommendation contains:
1. **What is weak or missing** — tied to specific answers, flags, or evidence gaps.
2. **Why it matters** — tied to this criterion's role for a profitable social enterprise.
3. **What specific action to take** — concrete, feasible, criterion-specific.
4. **What evidence would demonstrate improvement** — named evidence types.

Each recommendation is also tagged:
- **Type:** *Performance improvement* (change the enterprise) or *Evidence strengthening* (prove what is already claimed) or *Information completion* (answer U/ND items).
- **Dimension:** Commercial / Social / Both.
- **Source trigger:** the answer codes, flags, or evidence gaps that produced it.

Recommendations are generated from the trigger tables in each criterion section. They must not introduce facts about the market, sector, or location that were not provided (no invented statistics or benchmarks). Recommendations never change scores; a recommendation marked completed by the user does not raise any score [APPROVED].

## 5. Shared Principles and Rules

### 5.1 Question Bank Rules [APPROVED]
1. The eight criteria are fixed.
2. The 32 core questions and their answer options are fixed.
3. No new questions are generated during evaluation.
4. Every criterion receives an independent assessment.
5. Commercial and social dimensions are distinguished.
6. "لسه مش عارفين" does not mean the idea is weak or that its score is zero; the information is unavailable and requires verification.
7. Founder expectations are distinguished from information supported by evidence.
8. Scores and weights are not finalised before the scoring methodology is designed and approved.

The only approved follow-up question is the Large Market evidence follow-up. Evidence prompts suggested for other criteria in this document are **[PROPOSED]**, are **not** part of the approved question bank, and must not be shown to users without owner approval.

### 5.2 Evaluation Principles [APPROVED]
1. Assessment, evidence confidence, and recommendations are separate outputs.
2. Weak evidence does not automatically mean weak performance.
3. Strong evidence does not automatically mean strong performance.
4. Missing information does not automatically mean failure.
5. "لسه مش عارفين" does not automatically mean zero.
6. Estimates are labelled as estimates.
7. Assumptions are never presented as verified facts.
8. Conflicting evidence is explicitly flagged.
9. Recommendations are actionable and criterion-specific.
10. The same strength or weakness is not counted in more than one criterion (Section 14).
11. No evidence or external market facts are invented.
12. User-provided information is distinguished from independently verified evidence.
13. Commercial and social dimensions are evaluated distinctly.
14. A strength in one dimension does not compensate for a weakness in the other.
15. Grants and donations alone are never treated as proof of profitability, recurring revenue, or sustainability.

### 5.3 Treatment of Grants and Donations [APPROVED principle; application PROPOSED]
- Commercial-dimension questions refer to sales, customers, prices, and purchases. Income from grants or donations is not a sale, not a customer, and not a margin.
- If evidence or free text shows that costs are covered or growth is funded by grants/donations, the commercial reading must reflect the **commercial** position only, and the dependency is flagged ("Grant-dependent" flag).
- **Paid contracts** with NGOs, associations, or government bodies (LM Q1 options) for delivering a product or service **are** commercial revenue. Unconditional grants are not. When the nature is unclear, the item is flagged for clarification.

### 6.2 Criterion Interpretation

**What it measures.** The size of the opportunity on both sides of the enterprise:
- **Commercial:** who pays, and how many potential paying customers exist in the target geography.
- **Social:** who suffers from the problem, and how many people are affected in the target area — the size of the **actual social need**, not the number the project can currently serve [APPROVED principle].

**Why it matters.** A profitable social enterprise needs enough paying demand to sustain a business, and enough real need to justify the mission and allow impact to grow. A small market caps revenue; a small or unclear need caps impact.

**Strong performance looks like:** a clearly identified payer group; a potential customer population that is large relative to the target geography and the value of each customer; a clearly identified beneficiary group; a large affected population in the target area; both estimates tied to a stated geography and supported by data.

**Weak performance looks like:** very small potential customer and affected populations in the stated geography, with an unclear payer or beneficiary group.

**Considered under this criterion:** payer type; potential customer count; beneficiary group; affected-population count; target geography; basis of estimates; (where provided as evidence) prevalence and severity of the problem.

**Not considered here:** whether customers buy repeatedly (C3); price, margin, or willingness to pay (C2); whether the project can reach customers (C6); how many people the project currently serves (capacity is not need); whether impact has been demonstrated (C7).

**Distinction between dimensions.** The number of potential **customers** (Q2) and the number of **people affected** (Q4) are different quantities and must never be substituted for each other. In some models payers and beneficiaries are the same people (individuals paying for a solution to their own problem); in others they differ entirely (companies or government paying for services delivered to beneficiaries). Q1 vs Q3 reveals which model applies.

**Approved Large Market principles applied [APPROVED]:**
1. Need size is assessed, not current serving capacity → Q4 is read as total affected population in the target area.
2. Need assessment is separated from evidence confidence → Q4 band produces the reading; the evidence follow-up produces confidence.
3. Absolute population, prevalence, and severity are distinguished → Q4 measures absolute population only; prevalence and severity are not captured by any approved question; per D-28 they are assessed from the existing questions and available evidence only, and when evidence is missing this is flagged as an evidence gap — never estimated or invented by AI.
4. Target population and geography are considered together → bands are interpreted relative to the target country and area recorded in basic project information (§4.0.1). Geography is project context, not an evaluation question.
5. Weak evidence does not prove small need → an "impression" basis lowers confidence, never the size reading.
6. Unknown does not receive zero.
7. Recommendations explain how to improve and what evidence is needed.

### 6.4 Combined Interpretation and Assessment Logic

**Commercial reading (anchor Q2, qualified by Q1):**
- Q2 band is read **in the context of Q1 and the geography**. A B1/B2 band with Q1 = companies or government can still indicate a meaningful market; the same band with Q1 = individuals indicates a small consumer market.

**Social reading (anchor Q4, qualified by Q3):**

**Weak:** Q2 and Q4 in the lowest bands, with no indication that customer value or prevalence compensate within the criterion's meaning.
**Uncertainty:** Q2/Q4 = U; evidence basis U; geography not stated.
**Potential requiring validation:** High bands supported only by "impression" or "observations".

**Material factors:** payer type (value per customer), geography, the evidence basis, and payer/beneficiary model.

**Important combinations:**

### 7.2 Criterion Interpretation

**What it measures.**
- **Commercial:** the gap between selling price and direct cost (gross margin), and whether that margin is enough to cover operating expenses and fund growth.
- **Social:** whether beneficiaries receive income that is fair relative to their time, effort, and required investment.

**Why it matters.** Gross margin is the source from which a social enterprise pays its overheads, invests in growth, and funds its social mission without permanent subsidy. On the social side, an enterprise that is profitable while paying beneficiaries little for heavy effort is not delivering fair value to them.

**Strong:** price well above direct cost; margin covers expenses with clear growth room; beneficiaries earn regular, adequate income with a good return on their time and effort.
**Weak:** price at or below direct cost; margin does not cover costs; beneficiaries earn little for heavy effort or have no clear income mechanism.

**Considered:** price vs direct cost; coverage of expenses and growth; adequacy of beneficiary income relative to time, effort, and training/production cost.
**Not considered:** market size (C1); repeat frequency of revenue (C3); continuity/regularity of beneficiary income over time (C3 Q4 — see Section 14); supply cost stability (C5); grants (never margin).

**Gross margin vs net profit.** Q1 is a gross-margin question (direct cost only). Q2 extends to whether the margin covers expenses and growth — a margin-sufficiency question, not a net-profit measurement. A high gross margin with insufficient coverage indicates high overheads or low volume, not a gross-margin weakness.

**Dimension distinction.** Enterprise profitability (Q1–Q2) and beneficiary income fairness (Q3–Q4) are independent: a profitable enterprise does not automatically provide fair income to beneficiaries [APPROVED guidance].

### 8.2 Criterion Interpretation

**What it measures.**
- **Commercial:** whether revenue repeats from the same customers (repeat purchase, subscription, contracts, add-ons) and whether customer need recurs.
- **Social:** whether recurring demand translates into continuous work opportunities and continuous income for beneficiaries over time.

**Why it matters.** Recurring revenue makes income predictable, lowers acquisition cost per sale, and allows planning. For beneficiaries, continuity of work and income is what turns participation into a livelihood rather than a one-off activity.

**Strong:** regular repeat purchases or ongoing contracts driven by continuous need; continuous work and regular income for beneficiaries.
**Weak:** one-off purchases with no recurring need; beneficiaries get one-time income or no continuity mechanism.

**Considered:** customer relationship after first purchase; recurrence of need; link between demand recurrence and beneficiary work; regularity of beneficiary income over time.
**Not considered:** number of customers (C1); margin per sale or adequacy of income amount (C2); sales channels (C6); beneficiary independence after founders leave (C8).

**Recurring revenue ≠ recurring grants or donations [APPROVED].** Regular donor funding does not count as recurring revenue. Ongoing paid service contracts with organisations do.

**Dimension distinction.** Recurring revenue for the enterprise does not guarantee continuous work for beneficiaries (e.g., if production is batched or outsourced). Both are read separately.

### 10.2 Criterion Interpretation

**What it measures.**
- **Commercial:** the enterprise's supply chain reliability — where inputs come from, supplier alternatives, and the ability to provide materials, equipment, transport, and production regularly.
- **Social:** beneficiaries' access to the resources they need to participate, and their ability to keep working without permanent dependence on the project team.

**Why it matters.** Reliable supply keeps production continuous and costs stable (template note: "regularly and at stable cost; what if one supplier is late?"). For beneficiaries, access to inputs determines whether they can participate at all; dependence on the project team for every input limits scale and sustainability.

**Strong:** multiple local suppliers with alternatives; all elements available regularly; beneficiaries access resources nearby and can deal with suppliers themselves.
**Weak:** imported or hard-to-reach inputs; fundamental obstacles; beneficiaries cannot access resources and depend entirely on others.

**Considered:** supplier location and redundancy; regularity of materials, equipment, transport, production; beneficiary access (distance, cost, delivery); beneficiary self-sufficiency in obtaining resources.
**Not considered:** cost of inputs as margin (C2) — though cost stability is a supply concern; team relationships as an asset (C4); overall operational independence after founders leave (C8 — C5 Q4 is limited to **resources**).

**Distinction [APPROVED guidance]:** enterprise supply chain reliability (Q1–Q2) is separate from beneficiaries' access to resources (Q3–Q4).

**Applicability note.** For service or digital enterprises with few physical inputs, "resources" include equipment, tools, venues, connectivity, and transport (ISSUE-10).

* لا توجد خطة واضحة لضمان الاستمرار.

### 13.2 Criterion Interpretation

**What it measures (social-enterprise interpretation).**
- **Commercial:** whether there are organisations that could adopt, operate, partner in, or scale the project, and what continuity/expansion pathway is envisaged.
- **Social:** whether beneficiaries can manage the activities and continue working and earning without the founders.

**Why it matters.** Alan's original criterion asks whether there are clear, large potential buyers — i.e., whether value created can be realised and the venture can outlive its founders' direct involvement. In a social enterprise, the goal is **continuity of the business and the mission**, not necessarily a conventional financial sale. Beneficiary independence is the social form of a successful exit.

**Strong:** identified organisations that have already shown interest; a defined continuity/expansion pathway; beneficiaries able to manage independently and continue earning without founders.
**Weak:** no known adopters, no continuity vision; beneficiaries depend on founders and work stops without them.

**Considered:** adoption/partnership counterparts; continuity or expansion pathway; beneficiary management capacity; continuation of work and income without founders.
**Not considered:** current paying customers (C1/C6) — even if an organisation is both a customer and a potential adopter, its role here is adoption/continuity; beneficiary skills for production (C4); beneficiary resource sourcing (C5 Q4); recurring income patterns (C3).

**Mission preservation [APPROVED guidance].** Maximising a conventional financial exit is not assumed to be the objective. Pathways that transfer the project to an organisation must be read with attention to whether the social mission and beneficiary benefits are preserved. A pathway that may drop the mission is flagged ("Mission-preservation check"), not rewarded.

**Template conflict.** The template names this criterion "استراتيجية الخروج/ الاستتثمار" (exit/investment); the approved bank names it "استراتيجية الخروج والاستمرار" (exit and continuity) (ISSUE-01).

## 14. Criterion Boundaries and Avoiding Double-Counting

### 14.1 What Each Criterion Owns

| Criterion | Owns (commercial) | Owns (social) | Does NOT own |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 Large Market | Who pays; how many potential customers | Who is affected; how many are affected | Repeat, margin, channels, impact proof |
| C2 High Gross Margins | Price vs direct cost; coverage of expenses/growth | **Adequacy/fairness** of beneficiary income vs effort | Regularity of income over time (C3) |
| C3 Recurring Revenue | Repeat purchase; recurring need | **Continuity** of work and income over time | Income amount/fairness (C2) |
| C4 Leverage Our Capabilities | Team's existing capabilities and fit | Beneficiaries' existing skills and readiness to participate | Supply operations (C5), selling (C6), future management (C8) |
| C5 Supply Chain | Supplier sources and regular provision | Beneficiary **access to resources**, independence **in resources** | Overall operational independence (C8) |
| C6 Direct Customer Access | Channels and intermediary dependence | Beneficiary participation in value stages and market | Story appeal (C7), market size (C1) |
| C7 PR & Communications | Story clarity and appeal | Beneficiary voice; demonstrability of impact | Actual sales (C6/C3), need size (C1) |
| C8 Exit Strategy | Adopters, partners, continuity pathway | Beneficiary **management** and **continuation without founders** | Production skills (C4), resource sourcing (C5) |

### 14.2 Known Overlaps and the Rule Applied

| Overlap | Rule [PROPOSED] |
|---|---|
| C2 Q3 (income nature: regular / recurring / intermittent) vs C3 Q4 (income over time) | C2 reads **adequacy relative to effort**; C3 reads **regularity/continuity**. In C2, regularity-related wording informs context only; the regularity signal is owned by C3. Inconsistencies are flagged as cross-criterion checks (ISSUE-11). |
| C4 Q3/Q4 (beneficiary skills/readiness) vs C6 Q4 (market ability) vs C8 Q3 (management ability) | C4 = production/execution skills today; C6 = selling/customer interaction; C8 = managing and deciding in the future. Same beneficiary group, different capability. |
| C5 Q4 (resource independence) vs C8 Q4 (continuation without founders) | C5 = obtaining inputs; C8 = whole operation and income. A resource dependency is counted in C5 only. |
| C1 Q2 (customer count) vs C3 (repeat) | Size vs frequency. |
| C6 Q1 (channels) vs C7 Q2 (appeal) | Route to customers vs interest generated. Attention never counts as access. |
| C7 Q4 (impact demonstrability) vs C1 Q4 (need size) | How clearly change can be shown vs how large the need is. |
| C4 Q1 relationships vs C5 suppliers vs C6 channels | C4 = relationship as a team asset; C5/C6 = operational arrangement. A supplier relationship counted as an asset in C4 is not re-credited in C5; C5 assesses supply reliability. |
| C1 Q1 (government/NGO payers) vs C8 Q1 (adopters) | Paying customer vs future adopter/operator. |
| Grant dependency (C2, C3) | Flagged once at enterprise level and in the criterion where it is observed; not counted as a separate weakness in each criterion. |

### 14.3 General Double-Counting Rules [PROPOSED]
1. Each fact is assessed in the criterion that owns its aspect (14.1).
2. Facts may be **used as context** or **consistency checks** in other criteria, but not as performance signals there.
3. A single evidence item may support confidence in several criteria only for the aspect it actually evidences (e.g., a sales record evidences existence of paying customers (C1), a channel (C6), and repeat purchase (C3) only if it shows repeat buyers).
4. Cross-criterion contradictions are flagged once at project level and referenced in each affected criterion.

## 18. Framework Summary

- **Eight fixed criteria**, each with a Commercial (Q1–Q2) and Social (Q3–Q4) dimension; 32 approved core questions plus the Large Market evidence follow-up, reproduced exactly.
- **Answers → codes** (S+, S, L, W, X, ND, U, C, B1–B5). "لسه مش عارفين" is U: an information gap, never a negative signal. Stated absence (X) and not-yet-determined (ND) are distinct from U.
- **Codes → dimension readings** through criterion-specific anchor/qualifier rules and listed contradictions. Answers are interpreted together, never summed.
- **Evidence confidence is separate** from performance: evidence classes EV-1…EV-6; answers are founder statements; documented ≠ verified; grants are never commercial evidence.
- **Criterion outcomes** (seven labels) always show both dimension readings and confidence; a strong dimension never hides a weak one (Imbalanced outcome).
- **Recommendations** follow criterion-specific trigger tables and always state what is weak, why it matters, the action, and the evidence of improvement, typed as performance, evidence, or information.
- **Boundaries** between criteria are fixed (Section 14) to avoid double-counting.
- **No numbers** have been assigned. Phase 3 will design scoring on top of these readings.
- **Project context (v1.1):** target country and area are basic project information used to interpret Large Market. The beneficiary model decides which social dimensions apply; inapplicable ones are **Not Applicable** — never weak, never zero — with safeguards (model-conflict check, C1/C7 social always applicable, social-coverage and social-impact flags).
- **Open issues:** 16 logged; resolved by owner decision: ISSUE-06, 07, 08, 12, 13, 14; ISSUE-16 partly. ISSUE-11 must be confirmed before the Scoring Specification is finalised.
- **Phase 3 decisions applied (v1.3):** every question answered before submission (D-24); single-select (D-27); prevalence/severity from evidence only (D-28); evidence guidance for C2–C8 (D-29); N/A excluded from calculation with Social Coverage shown (D-25); confidence reported per criterion and overall, separate from scores (D-26).
